# Cooking Threshold

## The Wrangham Hypothesis

Richard Wrangham's "cooking hypothesis" establishes that control of fire and cooked food is the **foundational technology of human cognitive evolution**:

- Cooking allowed gut reduction and brain expansion
- Created pair-bonding, marriage, sexual division of labor
- Extended the waking day to 16 hours (double other primates)
- Fire created the **inside/outside boundary**: those with fire vs. those without

## The Ebu Gogo as Non-Cooking Hominins

The Ebu Gogo explicitly "ate everything raw." They:
- Lacked fire mastery
- Lacked clothing production
- Lacked symbolic self-representation
- Could not model deceptive intent (accepted the palm fiber trap)

They were **a parallel hominin lineage that never made the cooking transition**, and thus never developed the social, neurological, and temporal structures cooking makes possible.

## Fire as Ontological Wedge

The extermination method is the critical tell. The villagers used **fire** — the one technology the Ebu Gogo couldn't master. The message encoded in the method:

> "You cannot share our fire, so we will use it to destroy you."

This is not myth. This is **oral history of technological genocide**.

## Cognitive Consequences of Cooking

| Without Cooking | With Cooking |
|-----------------|--------------|
| Large gut, small brain | Reduced gut, expanded brain |
| No pair-bonding | Marriage, family structure |
| No fire = no night activity | 16-hour waking day |
| No food sharing | Reciprocal altruism |
| No symbolic culture | Language, ritual, religion |

The Ebu Gogo were on the wrong side of this threshold. They were **too similar to tolerate and too different to integrate**.
