# Bernoulli's Principle in Domestic Environments

## The Physics

Bernoulli's principle states that for an inviscid flow, an increase in fluid velocity occurs simultaneously with a decrease in pressure or potential energy:

$$P + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 + \rho gh = \text{constant}$$

## Application: Bedroom Fan Placement

### The Problem
- AC vent produces cold air at fixed rate
- Fan placement determines convective efficiency
- Goal: maximize thermal comfort with minimum energy

### Optimal Configuration

| Placement | Mechanism | Efficiency |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Near cold door | Induces pressure differential, entrains cold air | HIGH |
| Pointed at bed | Direct convective cooling | MEDIUM |
| Pointed up | Ceiling circulation, poor entrainment | LOW |
| Center room | Turbulent mixing, no directional advantage | MEDIUM |

### The Convective Current

When a fan is placed near a cold air source (bathroom door):

1. Fan accelerates air → local pressure drops (Bernoulli)
2. Pressure differential draws cold air from bathroom
3. Entrained cold air mixes with room air
4. Convective loop established: cold in, warm out

This is identical to the **Venturi effect** in fluid dynamics — the fan acts as a pump, the door as a nozzle.

### Energy Principle

The system operates as a **heat engine in reverse**:
- Input: Electrical energy (fan) + Thermal gradient (AC)
- Output: Directed convective flow
- Efficiency: Proportional to pressure differential squared

### Practical Recommendation

Place the fan **at the threshold** of the cold zone, angled to draw air across the room. This maximizes the pressure differential and creates the strongest convective loop.
